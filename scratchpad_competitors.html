Where the gap actually is

Every platform above requires an account, and most collect player/parent PII to run attendance, messaging, or payments. The one close analog on the simplicity axis — RosterPro — still centers on RSVP/scheduling, not a shared drill-based plan.

What the field converges on

  • Account + login for every coach and often every parent
  • Attendance, messaging, and payments bundled in, whether needed or not
  • Monetizes via subscriptions once a team grows past the free tier
  • "Multi-sport" mostly means separate drill libraries behind one login

What sports-training-planner already does differently

  • Zero accounts — public read, passcode-only writes, nobody signs up
  • Stores no PII at all, not even names (see PRIVACY.md) — nothing to leak, nothing to migrate for GDPR
  • One shared calendar per group, not a bundled suite nobody asked for
  • Genuinely free — no tier to hit, no upgrade prompt

Read for the multi-sport milestone

"Support more sports" alone doesn't differentiate — six competitors already claim it. The defensible angle is the only multi-sport planner that needs no account and stores no personal data, aimed squarely at volunteer coaches who'd rather text a passcode than onboard a platform.

Concretely: generalize the schema (per the open milestone), but keep every generalization compatible with the zero-signup / zero-PII model — that's the one property nothing in this table has, and it's cheap to keep as the sport list grows.
